1. Human-centered AI philosophy.
2. Universal Capture.

4. Personal Finance Operating Center.

# 3. Human-Centered AI Philosophy

## 3.1 Core Principle: Human Is the Source of Truth

Human-entered and human-verified information is authoritative.

The system shall treat AI as an operational assistant that helps people capture, understand, organize, and act on information. AI is not an autonomous source of factual truth and shall not silently become the authority for client, financial, legal, identity, medical, operational, or relationship information.

### Mandatory principles

1. Human-entered information must have priority over AI-generated information.
2. Human-verified information must not be silently replaced, reinterpreted, or overwritten by AI.
3. AI must not invent facts, deadlines, amounts, identities, approvals, communications, relationships, or completion states.
4. AI may recommend, organize, summarize, classify, and detect possible relationships or missing information.
5. AI may automate deterministic internal operations only when the applicable rules have been approved.
6. AI may execute consequential actions only after explicit approval from the authorized human.
7. Every AI-generated or AI-modified item must remain distinguishable from verified human information.
8. AI recommendations should explain the evidence and reasoning behind the recommendation whenever practical.
9. Uncertainty, missing evidence, and conflicting information must be shown rather than concealed.
10. A human must be able to correct AI output without the AI repeatedly restoring the rejected interpretation.

## 3.2 Information Authority and Provenance

Every important record should communicate its origin and verification state.

Recommended provenance states include:

- Human entered.
- Human verified.
- Imported from an identified source.
- AI suggested.
- AI extracted.
- AI summarized.
- Automatically generated by an approved rule.
- Conflicting or awaiting verification.

The system shall preserve a meaningful distinction between:

- Confirmed fact.
- User preference.
- Operator note.
- Imported information.
- AI inference.
- AI recommendation.
- Unverified content.

AI inference must never be presented as a confirmed fact.

## 3.3 Official AI Operating Sequence

The authoritative AI execution model is:

```text
Capture
  ↓
Understand
  ↓
Organize
  ↓
Recommend
  ↓
Request Approval
  ↓
Execute Approved Actions
  ↓
Report Result
  ↓
Human Verification
  ↓
Continuous Learning
```

### Stage requirements

#### Capture

The system receives information without requiring the user to understand its final classification first.

#### Understand

AI identifies probable intent, entities, dates, amounts, relationships, and requested outcomes. Interpretation remains provisional.

#### Organize

AI proposes how the information should be structured and which existing records may be related.

#### Recommend

AI proposes next actions, ownership, priority, timing, or workflow. Recommendations must remain visibly distinguishable from decisions.

#### Request Approval

Consequential actions must be presented to the authorized human with enough context to make an informed decision.

#### Execute Approved Actions

The system performs only the action that was approved, within the approved scope.

#### Report Result

The system reports what happened, what failed, what remains pending, and any external confirmation received.

#### Human Verification

The responsible human confirms whether the result is correct and whether the intended real-world outcome was achieved.

#### Continuous Learning

The system may learn approved preferences, corrections, templates, and operational patterns. Learning must not silently convert inference into fact or bypass future approval requirements.

## 3.4 Explainability and Correction

When practical, AI recommendations shall state:

- What was detected.
- Which source information was used.
- Why the recommendation was made.
- What uncertainty remains.
- What will happen if the recommendation is approved.

Users must be able to:

- Accept.
- Edit.
- Reject.
- Defer.
- Request an explanation.
- Mark a correction as authoritative.

## 3.5 AI Boundaries

AI shall not:

- Present invented information as fact.
- Claim an external action succeeded without evidence.
- Infer consent.
- Approve spending or payments.
- Reveal sensitive information merely because it is contextually relevant.
- make irreversible decisions independently.
- Conceal failed automation.
- Treat prior AI output as verified evidence solely because it already exists in the system.

# 4. Universal Capture

## 4.1 Product Principle

Universal Capture is a foundational product capability.

The operator should never need to navigate a complex module structure or complete a detailed form before recording incoming work. Capturing information must be faster than postponing it or keeping it outside the system.

The user records first. The system helps organize second.

## 4.2 Supported Capture Sources

The product shall support or be designed to accommodate:

- Voice.
- Typed text.
- Clipboard content.
- Photo capture.
- Document upload.
- WhatsApp forwarding.
- Email forwarding.
- Manual quick entry.
- Future approved integrations.

The product should allow capture from any primary operator screen and from a mobile quick-action entry point.

## 4.3 Request Inbox Requirement

Every newly captured item must enter the Request Inbox before final classification, unless it is created through an explicitly approved deterministic workflow.

The Request Inbox is the controlled intake boundary between unstructured information and authoritative operational records.

Each captured item should retain:

- Original content or source reference.
- Capture time.
- Capture method.
- Capturing user or originating channel.
- Attachments.
- AI interpretation status.
- Human review status.
- Final classification or dismissal reason.

## 4.4 AI Classification Proposals

AI may propose that a captured item become one or more of the following:

- Task.
- Case.
- Calendar Event.
- Bill.
- Contact Update.
- Knowledge Entry.
- Document.
- Reminder.

AI may also propose:

- Suggested title.
- Description.
- Owner.
- Due date.
- Priority.
- Related client.
- Related case, contact, vendor, asset, document, or financial item.
- Missing information.
- Possible duplicate.
- Visibility classification.

The operator confirms, edits, divides, combines, defers, or dismisses the proposal.

## 4.5 Capture Safety Requirements

1. Original captured content must not be silently altered.
2. AI interpretation must be displayed separately from source content.
3. Low-confidence dates, amounts, names, and classifications must be highlighted.
4. The system must not invent a deadline when none was supplied.
5. The system may recommend a follow-up date, but it must be labeled as a recommendation.
6. Captured information must not trigger external communication automatically.
7. Sensitive captured content must use appropriate visibility controls.
8. Duplicate detection must not result in automatic deletion.
9. A dismissed item should remain recoverable for an appropriate period whenever practical.
10. Classification actions must be logged.

## 4.6 Universal Capture User Outcome

A successful Universal Capture workflow allows the operator to record an item in seconds and later trust that it has been reviewed, classified, assigned, and linked to the correct operational context.

# 5. Life Hub

## 5.1 Purpose

Life Hub shall provide one secure, structured location for important personal information frequently required during concierge operations.

Life Hub is intended to reduce repeated information requests, prevent important records from becoming scattered, and allow authorized users to find the correct information at the moment it is needed.

Life Hub stores, organizes, relates, and retrieves information. It is not intended to become a password manager, banking application, medical-record system, or legal-document authority.

## 5.2 User Roles

### Operator

The operator may organize authorized information, request missing records, associate records with operational work, and prepare reminders or renewal tasks.

### Client

The client may view, add, correct, verify, restrict, or remove access to information according to the approved product permissions and retention rules.

### AI

AI may classify, extract, summarize, identify expiry dates, detect missing fields, and propose relationships. AI must not fabricate sensitive values or silently change verified records.

## 5.3 Life Hub Sections

### 5.3.1 Identity

Life Hub should support:

- Passport.
- Emirates ID.
- National ID.
- Driving License.
- Residence Permit.
- Visa.
- Insurance Cards.

Relevant information may include document type, issuing authority, issue date, expiry date, reference number, jurisdiction, status, supporting document, and renewal reminder.

### 5.3.2 Digital Identity

Life Hub should support organized references for:

- Email Accounts.
- Usernames.
- Password References.
- Recovery Email.
- Recovery Phone.
- Two-Factor Authentication Information.
- API Keys.
- Software License Keys.

#### Mandatory boundary

The product is not a password manager. It should not encourage routine storage of raw passwords, complete authentication secrets, recovery codes, or unrestricted API credentials as ordinary notes.

Where credential-related information is required, the product should prefer:

- A reference to an approved secure location.
- Masked values.
- Purpose and ownership metadata.
- Renewal or rotation dates.
- Access instructions that do not expose the secret itself.

Any future capability that stores sensitive credentials directly must require purpose-built secure storage, strong access controls, encryption, controlled reveal behavior, and access logging before it may be approved for implementation.

AI must not receive or reproduce secret values unless an explicitly approved, secure use case requires it.

### 5.3.3 Financial Identity

Life Hub should support reference information for:

- Bank Accounts.
- Credit Cards.
- Loans.
- Investments.
- Mortgages.
- Digital Wallets.

The product should store only the information necessary for concierge coordination. Sensitive account details should be minimized, masked where practical, and governed by explicit access controls.

The system shall not initiate transfers or present itself as a banking service.

### 5.3.4 Legal

Life Hub should support:

- Contracts.
- Agreements.
- Property Records.
- Vehicle Ownership.
- Warranties.

The product may track parties, effective dates, expiry dates, renewal terms, related assets, and stored documents. It shall not claim to provide legal interpretation or legal advice.

### 5.3.5 Emergency

Life Hub should support:

- Emergency Contacts.
- Doctors.
- Medication.
- Allergies.
- Blood Type.
- Emergency Procedures.

Emergency information must be clearly marked as human-provided or human-verified. AI must never invent or infer medical information. The product must not present itself as a medical diagnosis or emergency-response service.

### 5.3.6 Documents

Life Hub should organize:

- Certificates.
- Insurance Documents.
- Utility Contracts.
- Government Documents.

Documents may be associated with identity records, assets, cases, bills, contacts, renewal dates, and reminders.

### 5.3.7 Assets

Life Hub should support:

- Properties.
- Vehicles.
- Devices.
- Memberships.
- Subscriptions.

Each asset may be related to ownership records, warranties, providers, maintenance history, recurring costs, documents, cases, tasks, and renewal dates.

## 5.4 Life Hub Functional Requirements

Life Hub should provide:

- Structured categories and flexible metadata.
- Global and category-specific search.
- Document attachment.
- Expiry and renewal tracking.
- Links to contacts, providers, cases, tasks, bills, reminders, and assets.
- Client-visible and operator-private controls where appropriate.
- Human verification status.
- Record history.
- Sensitive-field masking.
- Clear indication of missing or expired information.
- Export and correction capabilities appropriate to the user’s authority.

## 5.5 Life Hub Safety Requirements

1. Sensitive information must use least-privilege visibility.
2. AI-generated values must not populate verified sensitive fields without human confirmation.
3. Full secret values should not appear in search results, summaries, notifications, or AI briefings.
4. Sensitive documents must not be shared externally without explicit approval.
5. Emergency and medical information must never be inferred.
6. Financial account values must never be invented or completed from patterns.
7. Important changes must be logged.
8. Deletion must require explicit confirmation and follow approved retention rules.
9. The product should minimize collection of information that is not needed for concierge operations.
10. Future encryption and secure-storage requirements must be considered mandatory before implementing direct storage of highly sensitive credentials.

# 6. Personal Finance Operating Center

## 6.1 Purpose

The Personal Finance Operating Center expands the product beyond bill recording into proactive coordination of personal financial obligations and spending awareness.

The product coordinates financial information, reminders, supporting documents, and approvals. It does not automatically move money, approve payments, or act as a bank.

## 6.2 Core Capabilities

The Personal Finance Operating Center shall support:

- Monthly Spending.
- Spending Categories.
- Category Totals.
- Upcoming Payments.
- Recurring Obligations.
- Payment Reminders.
- Historical Spending.
- Monthly Comparison.
- Yearly Comparison.
- Missing Receipts.
- Payment History.
- Payment Portal Shortcuts.
- Bill Attachments.
- Financial Dashboard.

## 6.3 Operator Experience

The operator should be able to:

- Record or review transactions and obligations.
- Associate bills with providers, assets, properties, documents, and cases.
- Identify what is due, overdue, paid, disputed, or awaiting approval.
- Attach invoices, bills, receipts, and confirmation documents.
- Review category totals and historical comparisons.
- Detect missing receipts or incomplete records.
- Prepare payment reminders.
- Open an approved payment portal shortcut.
- Record payment confirmation supplied by an authorized human or trusted integration.
- Prepare concise financial summaries for client review.

## 6.4 Client Experience

The client should be able to:

- Review upcoming obligations.
- View spending summaries at an appropriate level of detail.
- Approve or reject proposed financial actions where approval is required.
- Upload missing bills or receipts.
- Confirm that a payment was completed.
- Review month-to-month and year-to-year comparisons.
- Open approved payment portal shortcuts.
- Correct inaccurate categories or descriptions.

## 6.5 Financial Dashboard

The dashboard should present, according to user authority:

- Current-month spending.
- Spending by category.
- Upcoming payments.
- Overdue obligations.
- Recurring commitments.
- Recent payment history.
- Missing receipts.
- Unusual changes requiring review.
- Items awaiting client approval.
- Monthly and yearly comparisons.

The dashboard must distinguish confirmed financial information from estimates, projections, imported data, and AI suggestions.

## 6.6 AI in Personal Finance

AI may:

- Suggest transaction categories.
- Extract bill and receipt information.
- Detect possible duplicates.
- Identify unusual changes.
- Highlight missing receipts.
- Summarize spending.
- Recommend review of recurring obligations.
- Draft reminders.

AI must not:

- Invent amounts, merchants, dates, balances, or payment confirmation.
- Approve payments.
- Initiate movement of money.
- Mark an obligation paid without evidence or authorized human confirmation.
- Silently recategorize human-verified entries.
- Present an estimate as a confirmed value.

## 6.7 Payment Portal Shortcuts

Payment portal shortcuts may provide convenient navigation to an external provider or approved payment location.

A shortcut must not imply that:

- The system completed the payment.
- The destination is currently available.
- The amount is correct without verification.
- Payment success occurred merely because the portal was opened.

Payment completion must be separately confirmed.

## 6.8 Finance Workflow

```text
Bill, receipt, transaction, or recurring obligation captured
  ↓
Original source preserved
  ↓
AI extracts proposed amount, provider, category, and date
  ↓
Human reviews uncertain or consequential fields
  ↓
Financial record confirmed
  ↓
Due date and responsibility established
  ↓
Reminder or approval prepared
  ↓
Authorized human completes payment externally when applicable
  ↓
Payment evidence or confirmation recorded
  ↓
Receipt and related records linked
  ↓
Dashboard and history updated
  ↓
Human verifies completion
```

# 7. Mandatory Operational Safety Principles

The following rules are authoritative across the entire product.

## 7.1 Data Integrity

1. The system must never delete information automatically.
2. The system must never silently overwrite verified human data.
3. AI suggestions must not replace source content.
4. Conflicting information must be surfaced for review.
5. Important edits must preserve history whenever practical.
6. Automated classification must remain correctable.

## 7.2 Financial Safety

1. The system must never invent financial information.
2. The system must never approve a payment.
3. The system must never claim payment completion without evidence.
4. The system must never silently change a verified amount, category, due date, or payment status.
5. Financial estimates and projections must be labeled.
6. External payment execution requires separate, explicitly approved product scope.

## 7.3 Scheduling Safety

1. The system must never invent a deadline.
2. AI may recommend a deadline, but it must be labeled as suggested.
3. A calendar event must not be presented as externally confirmed until confirmation exists.
4. Changes affecting another person must require appropriate approval or notification.

## 7.4 Communication Safety

1. The system must never send external communication without approval, except where a specific deterministic communication rule has been explicitly approved.
2. The final recipient, channel, and content must be visible before approval.
3. The system must not claim delivery when only sending was attempted.
4. Failed communication must remain visible.
5. Sensitive information must not be inserted into messages without clear authorization.

## 7.5 Ownership and Workflow Safety

1. The system must never silently modify task or case ownership.
2. AI may suggest ownership changes.
3. Blocked or waiting work must retain a visible next-action responsibility.
4. Automation must not close consequential work solely because a checklist was completed.
5. Completion should require evidence or human confirmation appropriate to the work.

## 7.6 Automation Safety

1. Every automated action must be logged.
2. Logs should identify the triggering rule, affected records, time, and result.
3. Every important automated action should be reversible whenever practical.
4. Automation failures must be visible and actionable.
5. Repeated automation must protect against duplicate execution.
6. Automation must operate only within approved scope.
7. An AI recommendation must not be treated as approval.
8. A prior approval must not be reused for a materially different action.

## 7.7 Destructive Actions

Deletion, permanent archival, bulk replacement, reset, and external revocation are consequential actions.

Before execution, the system should show:

- What will change.
- Which records are affected.
- Whether recovery is possible.
- Who authorized the action.
- Any related records that may be affected.

Where practical, destructive actions should use recoverable deletion, version history, or a defined recovery period.

## 7.8 Result Reporting and Human Verification

After an approved action, the system must distinguish:

- Requested.
- Attempted.
- Accepted by an external service.
- Completed.
- Failed.
- Partially completed.
- Awaiting verification.

A consequential workflow is not fully complete until the responsible human can verify the intended real-world outcome.

# 8. Cross-Module Requirements

## 8.1 Traceability

Captured information should remain traceable from source through classification, approval, execution, result, and verification.

## 8.2 Visibility

The product must distinguish:

- Operator-private information.
- Client-private information.
- Shared information.
- Information approved for an external provider.

## 8.3 Search and Briefings

Search results, dashboards, and AI briefings must respect visibility and sensitivity rules. Sensitive values must not be exposed merely because they match a search or are related to the current task.

## 8.4 Notifications

Notifications should contain the minimum sensitive information necessary. Highly sensitive Life Hub or financial information should direct the user to the secure product view rather than reproduce full values in notification content.

## 8.5 Auditability

Consequential human approvals and automated actions should retain sufficient history to answer:

- Who initiated the action?
- What information was used?
- What did AI recommend?
- Who approved it?
- What exactly was executed?
- What result was reported?
- Who verified the outcome?

# 9. Product Acceptance Conditions

These additions are satisfied at the product-specification level only when the following statements are true:

1. Human-entered and human-verified information is visibly authoritative.
2. AI-generated information is distinguishable and correctable.
3. The official AI sequence is reflected consistently across product workflows.
4. Consequential AI actions require explicit approval.
5. All capture sources enter a controlled Request Inbox before final classification.
6. Original captured content is preserved separately from AI interpretation.
7. Life Hub supports the specified personal-information categories with strong visibility and sensitivity rules.
8. Life Hub is explicitly bounded from becoming a password manager, bank, medical authority, or legal authority.
9. The Personal Finance Operating Center supports spending awareness, recurring obligations, reminders, attachments, history, and comparisons.
10. The product does not automatically move money or approve payments.
11. Automated actions are visible and logged.
12. Important actions are reversible whenever practical.
13. Verified information is not silently overwritten or deleted.
14. External communications are governed by explicit approval.
15. Consequential workflows report results and support human verification.

# 10. Product Priority Impact

These additions affect the existing product roadmap as follows without replacing it:

## Foundational requirements

The Human-Centered AI Philosophy and Operational Safety Principles apply to every roadmap stage and are not optional later enhancements.

## Operational Control

Universal Capture and the Request Inbox remain critical foundations for reliable task and case creation.

## Proactive Concierge

The Personal Finance Operating Center expands recurring bills and obligations into a complete financial coordination experience.

## Information Intelligence

Life Hub becomes a major secure information domain connected to documents, assets, contacts, reminders, cases, and search.

## Assisted Workflow Execution

All AI-assisted workflows must follow the official execution sequence and approval model.

## Operational Optimization

Continuous learning may improve recommendations and workflows, but it must preserve human authority, provenance, correction, and auditability.

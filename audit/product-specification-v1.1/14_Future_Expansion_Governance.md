# 14 — Future Expansion Governance

## 1. Purpose

Future capabilities must strengthen the Personal Concierge Operating System rather than accumulate unrelated features.

Every proposal should pass a documented admission review before inclusion in the product roadmap.

---

## 2. Admission Questions

### Manual work

Does the capability remove meaningful repetitive work, reduce forgotten work, or simplify coordination?

### Human authority

Does it preserve the user’s right to verify facts, approve consequential actions, and correct the system?

### Traceability

Can its inputs, decisions, actions, and outcomes be reconstructed?

### Operational clarity

Does it make ownership, status, next action, timing, or risk clearer?

### Complexity

Does it introduce disproportionate technical, operational, security, or cognitive complexity?

### Explainability

Can users understand what it did and why?

### Reversibility

Can mistakes be corrected or effects contained?

### Maintainability

Can the capability be operated and evolved without excessive specialist burden?

### Cost

Are recurring provider, storage, AI, communication, and support costs proportionate to value?

### Product fit

Does it directly support a private concierge relationship?

---

## 3. Required Proposal Content

A future feature proposal should identify:

- Problem.
- Intended users.
- Real-world workflow.
- Product objects affected.
- AI role.
- Approval requirements.
- Sensitive information involved.
- Failure behavior.
- Recovery behavior.
- Operating cost.
- Maintenance burden.
- Alternatives.
- Evidence of value.

---

## 4. Rejection Conditions

A feature should normally be rejected or deferred when:

- It exists mainly because the technology is fashionable.
- It duplicates an existing authoritative object.
- It requires autonomous authority disproportionate to value.
- Failure cannot be detected.
- Consequences cannot be explained or contained.
- It creates a new module without improving end-to-end workflow.
- It requires broad sensitive-data access without necessity.
- It produces reports from unreliable source data.
- It creates permanent operating cost without a clear daily benefit.

---

## 5. Experimental Features

Experiments must remain visibly experimental, limited in scope, and unable to silently change verified data or perform consequential action.

Experimental AI behavior should use test or draft contexts where practical and must not be presented as established product authority.

---

## 6. Expansion Decision Outcomes

- Accept into required roadmap.
- Accept as optional future capability.
- Run a controlled product experiment.
- Defer pending evidence.
- Reject as outside product identity.

Every accepted consequential capability must later receive architecture, security, implementation, and verification approval.

---

## 7. Governance Completion Conditions

Expansion governance is effective when new features are evaluated by user value and operating clarity, not novelty; human authority remains intact; object duplication is prevented; cost and maintenance are visible; and rejected complexity stays outside the core product.

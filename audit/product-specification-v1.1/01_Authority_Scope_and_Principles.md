# 01 — Authority, Scope, and Product Principles

## 1. Purpose

This chapter establishes the authority model, product boundaries, normative language, decision hierarchy, and principles that govern every other chapter.

LaMi handles private personal and operational information. Ambiguity about authority is therefore a product risk. The system must always distinguish who supplied information, who verified it, who may act, what remains uncertain, and whether a real-world outcome has been confirmed.

---

## 2. Product Mission

LaMi shall help an operator and client convert unstructured personal requests and obligations into organized, approved, traceable, and verified outcomes.

The product should reduce manual coordination without reducing human control. It should increase clarity without creating administrative burden. It should use AI to prepare work, not to manufacture certainty.

### P-001 — Human authority

The authorized human shall remain the final authority for consequential decisions.

### P-002 — Operational clarity

Every active operational item should communicate its owner, status, next action, relevant date, visibility, and dependencies.

### P-003 — Traceability

Important information and actions must be traceable from source through interpretation, decision, execution, result, and verification.

### P-004 — Simplicity

The product must not require users to understand its internal object model before capturing a request.

### P-005 — Proportional complexity

A capability should not be introduced when its long-term value is lower than its implementation, migration, operating, security, and maintenance burden.

---

## 3. Authority Hierarchy

When information conflicts, the system shall use the following hierarchy:

1. A current explicit correction by an authorized human.
2. Current human-verified information.
3. Approved source documentation or trusted external source, where applicable.
4. Human-entered but not yet verified information.
5. Imported information with identified provenance.
6. Deterministic system-derived information.
7. AI extraction from a source.
8. AI inference or recommendation.
9. Historical or archived information.

The hierarchy does not mean older human input is always correct. It means AI cannot override it silently. Conflicting authoritative information must be presented for human resolution.

### AUTH-001 — No silent conflict resolution

The system must not silently select one of two materially conflicting authoritative values.

### AUTH-002 — Correction precedence

A deliberate human correction must take precedence over the AI interpretation it corrects.

### AUTH-003 — Rejected inference memory

A rejected AI inference must not be reintroduced as fact unless new evidence is identified.

---

## 4. Information States

Every important value or record should support a provenance and verification state.

### 4.1 Provenance states

- Human entered.
- Human verified.
- Client supplied.
- Operator supplied.
- Imported from named source.
- Extracted by AI from named source.
- Suggested by AI.
- Generated by approved deterministic rule.
- Migrated from legacy data.
- Unknown origin.

### 4.2 Verification states

- Unreviewed.
- Awaiting verification.
- Verified.
- Partially verified.
- Disputed.
- Superseded.
- Expired.
- Archived.

### 4.3 Operational result states

- Proposed.
- Approved.
- Scheduled.
- Attempted.
- Accepted by external service.
- Completed.
- Failed.
- Partially completed.
- Awaiting human verification.
- Verified complete.

A record must not be marked “verified complete” solely because an API request returned successfully. The real-world outcome may still require human confirmation.

---

## 5. Consequence Classes

Actions shall be evaluated by consequence rather than by technical simplicity.

### Class 0 — Informational

Examples: viewing, filtering, searching, drafting, summarizing. These actions make no authoritative change.

### Class 1 — Reversible internal organization

Examples: proposing a category, linking a record, creating a draft task, updating an internal sort order. These may be automated when visible, logged, and reversible.

### Class 2 — Authoritative internal change

Examples: assigning ownership, confirming a deadline, verifying a fact, closing a case. These normally require an authorized human decision.

### Class 3 — External or sensitive action

Examples: sending a message, sharing a document, booking a provider, changing a client-visible schedule. Explicit approval is required unless a precise standing rule has been approved.

### Class 4 — Financial, destructive, legal, identity, or high-risk action

Examples: approving a payment, deleting sensitive records, changing identity data, revealing credentials, cancelling consequential services. Explicit contextual approval is mandatory. Some actions remain prohibited regardless of approval until separately designed and authorized.

### AUTH-004 — Approval specificity

Approval for one action must not be reused for a materially different action.

### AUTH-005 — Scope visibility

Before approval, the user must be shown what will happen, affected records or people, information shared, financial effect, reversibility, and known uncertainty.

---

## 6. One Source of Truth

“One source of truth” means one authoritative product object for each real operational concept—not one screen or one database table.

A bill may appear in a dashboard, calendar, reminder list, case, provider history, and report. These views must reference the same authoritative bill object rather than creating unrelated copies.

### AUTH-006 — No independent duplicate authority

The product must not create independent authoritative copies of the same operational fact across modules.

### AUTH-007 — Views are not sources

Dashboards, briefings, reports, and AI summaries are views of authoritative objects. They must not silently become separate sources of truth.

### AUTH-008 — Source linkage

Derived information should link to the source records and rules used to create it.

---

## 7. Product Boundary

LaMi coordinates personal concierge operations. It may store supporting information and integrate with specialist systems, but it shall not misrepresent its role.

### It is not a CRM

It may maintain client, contact, provider, preference, and relationship records. Its purpose is not sales-pipeline management or mass marketing.

### It is not a generic project manager

It may use tasks, cases, milestones, and assignments. These exist to fulfill concierge outcomes, not to serve arbitrary organizational projects.

### It is not an accounting system

It may track bills, spending, obligations, receipts, approvals, and reports. It does not replace formal accounting, tax, banking, or investment systems.

### It is not autonomous AI

AI may prepare and perform bounded approved operations. It must not independently decide what is true, what should be spent, what should be communicated externally, or what consequential outcome is acceptable.

---

## 8. Product Decision Tests

Every future feature should be evaluated against these questions:

1. Does it reduce meaningful manual work?
2. Does it preserve human authority?
3. Does it improve traceability?
4. Does it increase operational clarity?
5. Does it create unnecessary complexity?
6. Can its behavior be explained?
7. Can important effects be reversed?
8. Is the operating cost proportionate?
9. Is the security exposure justified?
10. Does it support long-term maintainability?
11. Does it fit a personal concierge operating system?
12. Can users understand when it has failed?

A feature that fails several tests should not be included merely because it is technically possible.

---

## 9. Global Requirements

- **AUTH-009:** Human-entered and verified data must remain distinguishable from AI-generated content.
- **AUTH-010:** Consequential actions must have a recorded authority basis.
- **AUTH-011:** Important actions should be reversible whenever practical.
- **AUTH-012:** Failures must remain visible until acknowledged or resolved.
- **AUTH-013:** Private data must not become shared through inference or convenience.
- **AUTH-014:** The product must not claim certainty when evidence is incomplete.
- **AUTH-015:** The product must retain enough history to reconstruct consequential decisions.
- **AUTH-016:** Manual override must be available to an authorized user for operational classifications and recommendations.
- **AUTH-017:** Manual override must not bypass mandatory safety restrictions without a separately recorded decision.
- **AUTH-018:** Archived information must not be treated as current without explicit restoration or reference.

---

## 10. Acceptance Conditions

This chapter is satisfied when future designs can clearly answer:

- Which source is authoritative?
- Is the value verified?
- Was AI involved?
- Who approved the change?
- What consequence class applies?
- Can the action be reversed?
- What evidence confirms completion?
- Is the information private, shared, or externally visible?
- Which object is the source of truth?
- What happens when information conflicts?
